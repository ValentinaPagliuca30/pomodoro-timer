🍅 The Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method that uses a timer to break work into focused intervals (traditionally 25 minutes) separated by short breaks (5 minutes). Named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer used by its creator, Francesco Cirillo, this technique helps students maintain concentration, combat procrastination, and prevent burnout.

How it works: Focus for 25 minutes → Take a 5-minute break → Repeat. After 4 "pomodoros," take a longer 15-30 minute break.

About This Gallery

This project showcases 25 radically different design approaches for a Pomodoro timer, each exploring unique visual metaphors, interaction patterns, and moods. From sunsets to pizza slices, from batteries to growing plants—each version tells the story of time passing in its own creative way.

🏆 Final Version: v25 - Lunar Cycle

Why I chose v25:

After building 25 different versions, I picked the lunar cycle because it just works better for studying. The dark background doesn't strain your eyes, and watching the moon slowly fill up gives you a sense of progress without being distracting.

What makes it work:

  • Dark mode is easier on the eyes. When you're studying late at night or in a dark room, a bright screen kills your focus. The dark blue gradient background is gentle and helps you concentrate longer.
  • The moon metaphor makes sense. We all know what a full moon looks like. Watching it gradually reveal itself from shadow feels natural—you're not staring at an arbitrary progress bar, you're watching something familiar complete itself.
  • It's calm, not distracting. Some versions I made were too busy (like the aquarium with 20 fish swimming everywhere, or the Crossy Road one). The moon just sits there and slowly reveals itself. The stars twinkle a bit but they don't pull your attention away from your work.
  • Time feels like it's growing, not running out. With a countdown timer, you feel pressure. With the moon, you're building toward something complete. It's a subtle psychological difference but it matters when you're trying to focus.

The design process:

I started by making a bunch of wildly different versions—some minimal (v1), some playful (v5 tomato, v18 pizza), some complex (v25 aquarium). I wanted to explore everything before deciding.

What I learned: the more animations and colors you add, the more distracting it gets. The pizza getting eaten slice by slice is fun to look at for 30 seconds, but after that you just want to focus on your work. Same with the fish swimming—it's cool but your eyes keep following them.

The lunar cycle hits the sweet spot: it's visually interesting enough that you don't feel like you're staring at a boring countdown, but simple enough that it doesn't compete for your attention. Plus the dark theme just feels right for a study timer.

But honestly? I'm just a moon lover. There's something about it that keeps you connected to nature even when you're locked in your room studying for hours. When you're isolated at your desk, looking at something natural—something that's actually happening in the sky above you right now—makes it feel less lonely. The moon is simply beautiful, and it's always been my favorite. So choosing it for my final version wasn't just about functionality. It's what I genuinely want to look at while I study.
