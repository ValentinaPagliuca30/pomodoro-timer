25 Design Iterations - A Rapid Prototyping Journey

About This Project

This gallery showcases the design process of creating a Pomodoro study timer for students. Starting with a simple idea, I explored 25 radically different visual styles, layouts, and moods before converging on a final design.

The Journey: Wide exploration (v1-v15) → Narrowing down (v16-v20) → Refinement (v21-v25)

Final Version (v25)

Why v25 is the perfect choice:

After exploring 25 radically different design directions, v25 emerged as the ideal solution because it successfully combines all the best elements discovered throughout the journey:

  • Playful yet Professional: The bouncing tomato icon adds personality and makes studying feel less intimidating, while the clean iOS-inspired design keeps it polished and serious enough for academic work.
  • Perfect Color Harmony: The blue-purple gradient (from v17-v23 refinements) is both calming for concentration and energizing enough to stay motivated. It's easier on the eyes than the harsh neons (v9) but more engaging than pure monochrome (v13).
  • Full-Screen Focus: The large, centered timer dominates the screen (v18), making it impossible to miss. Unlike the sidebar approach (v10) or compact designs, this forces you to commit fully to the session.
  • Smooth Interactions: Subtle animations (v22) make the interface feel responsive and alive, without the overwhelming chaos of v15. Every button press feels satisfying.
  • Apple Aesthetic: Clean typography, generous spacing, and rounded corners create a familiar, trustworthy feel for students who use modern devices. It feels at home on any screen.

The wide exploration phase (v1-v15) taught me what doesn't work - brutalism is too harsh for long study sessions, retro is distracting, and pure minimalism can feel cold. The convergence phase (v16-v25) let me refine the winning combination into something that's both functional and delightful.
